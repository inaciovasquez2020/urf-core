# UCLP normalization correction

Source: [universal quantifier manuscript](../../papers/universal_quantifier_uclp.tex), blob b294a51a14db523751bc9088c8838b0b7dc9447b. PROVED_ON_PAPER for the counterexample and restricted simulation below; unrestricted normalization OPEN.

The tuple (q,h,a-,a0,a+) is not a sufficient state. Consider inputs 000 and 001, head at cell 0, same initial control, and a machine whose first transition moves right without changing the scanned symbol. The old windows agree (blank,0,0), but the new right neighbor at cell 2 differs. Hence no deterministic transition on the tuple alone simulates both runs. A two-sided tape also permits negative head positions: use -T_M(n)<=h<=T_M(n).

A corrected step-preserving simulation retains the entire persistent tape, control and head position. At time t, apply the machine transition to the scanned symbol and control, update only that tape cell, and move the head at most one cell. Induction on t proves exact configuration and output preservation. At most n+2T_M(n)+1 cells need storage, and a signed head coordinate uses O(log(T_M(n)+1)) bits. The local rule is radius one in tape geometry with control carried by the head; this is not a claim that the global state fits in a three-symbol window. For any fixed input distribution, the scanned-symbol transcript entropy grows by at most log2|Gamma| per emitted symbol, by the chain rule. This is a transcript-specific functional, not a bound on all information in the machine or its initial tape.

The quantifiers must be machine-specific: for every polynomial-time M there exists a polynomial horizon T_M. A single polynomial horizon fixed before all M cannot contain all literal step-preserving polynomial-time executions; machines can perform arbitrarily high polynomial numbers of steps before halting. This does not rule out other output-preserving simulations, which require separate proof. Likewise a fixed capacity constant independent of machine alphabet needs its own encoding argument.

For Word-RAM, retain persistent memory and specify address selection and the geometric representation. O(1) word accesses with O(log n)-bit words bound transferred bits, not distance between components. On a line of memory cells a register-directed access can reach an arbitrarily distant address. An access-dependent star representation, fixed geometric graph, and a simulation of pointer routing are distinct models; no constant-radius assertion follows without defining and validating the chosen geometry.

The historical normalization statements are superseded to this extent. No general URF admissibility, universal entropy-depth lower bound, or P versus NP conclusion is established. The original source is retained; no Lean declarations changed.
