## 1. Definitions and exact capacity
Fix a finite simple graph G, an oriented incidence boundary, field K of cardinality q (finite for entropy), Z=ker boundary. R_r is the span of circulations supported in induced closed graph-distance balls of integer radius r. S_k is the span of circulations supported on at most k edges. Observation O_r is restriction Z* -> R_r*. On any positive-mass fiber entropy is bounded by log |ker O_r|; uniform input on Z* attains it because every fiber is an affine translate of the kernel. Restriction is surjective by extension of functionals. Thus maximum conditional entropy C_G(r)=h_G(r) log q, where h_G(r)=dim Z/R_r. Uniform optimal exact reconstruction probability is q^(-h_G(r)) (each posterior is uniform; mixing guesses cannot improve success). This probability assertion is under the uniform prior only. Logs natural.

## 2. Sharp radius-support equivalence
R_r=S_(2r+1), over any field. A circulation decomposes into oriented simple cycles within its support: every nonzero circulation support contains an undirected cycle (a forest has a leaf and no nonzero circulation); subtract a scalar multiple of its oriented cycle chain to cancel a chosen nonzero coefficient, then induct on support size. Every simple cycle of length <=2r+1 fits into a radius-r ball about any of its vertices. Conversely every induced ball has a breadth-first spanning tree rooted at its center with depth <=r: a shortest path from the center stays in the ball. Each non-tree edge generates a fundamental cycle of length <=2r+1. Those cycles span by eliminating non-tree coefficients and using absence of tree circulations. Sum over centers. Sharp for r>=1: C_(2r+1) is locally generated, but C_(2r+2) has no cycle in any radius-r ball. At r=0 the identity holds but sharpness of the numerical cutoff does not (simple graphs have no cycles of length <=2).

R_r subset R_(r+1). R_0=0; R_D=Z for the maximum component diameter D (0 for empty graph), since a circulation restricts to a circulation in each component. C(r)-C(r+1)=dim(R_(r+1)/R_r) log q; these dimensions telescope to dim Z. Chordal graphs have R_1=Z by splitting cycles along chords.

## 3. Cycle basis and persistence
Greedy independent oriented simple cycles ordered by length give a minimum-total-length basis by linear matroid exchange; at each threshold k the selected prefix spans S_k. Every minimum basis has the same prefix ranks: if its short prefix fails to span S_k, express a short cycle outside that span in the basis and exchange a nonzero longer coefficient, strictly reducing weight. If lengths are l_i, C(r)=log q times #{i:l_i>2r+1}. The cycle-filling CW filtration attaches disks along all simple cycles of length <=2r+1; its H_1 is Z/R_r. An adapted basis gives intervals [0,floor(l_i/2)); sum_r C(r)=log q sum_i floor(l_i/2); first complete radius is max_i floor(l_i/2), zero for forests. Not a Rips filtration. Field dependence retained. Polynomial generating set: BFS tree at every vertex, all non-tree fundamental cycles; at each threshold retain cycles of length <=2r+1. The local ball BFS proof applies to restrictions of these global BFS trees. At most O(nm) candidates, exact rank computation over fixed finite field; no exponential cycle enumeration needed.

## 4. Edge perturbations
Insert e within a component. Cycle rank increases by one and R_r is nested under graph inclusion. C_new-C_old=(1-delta)log q where delta is the increase in local dimension. Thus C_new<=C_old+log q; bridging components leaves C unchanged by the short-cycle description. No absolute edge-count Lipschitz bound: K_(2,n) has h(1)=n-1; joining the two hubs gives n independent triangles spanning the new cycle space and h=0.
For old endpoint distance L, replace the new edge in chains by an oriented shortest path of length L. This linear map is identity on old cycles, hence surjects onto old Z; it sends new short cycles into S_(2r+L), which lies in R_(r+s) for s=ceil((L-1)/2), by the support decomposition above. Thus it induces a surjection of quotients: C_old(r+s)<=C_new(r). If L<=2r the fundamental cycle is short and delta>=1, improving the upper bound to C_old(r).

## 5. Single and multiple edges
For an intra-component e oriented u->v and k=2r+1, let P range over old simple u-v paths of length <=2r. If nonempty, choose P0. New short cycles are P-e; their span modulo old R is generated by P0-e plus P-P0. The former has nonzero new-edge coordinate, the latter lie in old Z. D_e=span of classes [P-P0] in Z/R; gain g_e=dim D_e. h_new=h_old-g_e; uniform reconstruction probability improves by factor q^g_e. If no short path exists h_new=h_old+1. Bridges unchanged. Optimal single eligible edge maximizes g_e.

General A: project augmented Z_A to coefficients on A by pi. Kernel is old Z. Let g=dim((R_A intersect Z)/R), u=dim(pi Z_A/pi R_A). Short exact sequence
0 -> Z/(R_A intersect Z) -> Z_A/R_A -> pi Z_A/pi R_A -> 0
gives h_A=h-g+u. If M columns generate R_A and N is its new-edge row projection, g=rank M-rank N-dim R. When each candidate has an old short path, short cycles with individual new-edge unit coordinates imply pi R_A=K^A and u=0, also for every subset. Hence recovery monotone on these candidate sets.

## 6. Optimization and interaction
Submodularity fails: G=C5 with edges e=02,f=03. Global and triangle ranks for none, e, f, both are respectively (1,0),(2,1),(2,1),(3,3) over every field (triangles independent by unique boundary edges). Gains 0,0,0,1 contradict submodularity. This refutes the generic submodular argument, not by itself every possible greedy approximation guarantee.
If every candidate has an old short path and every short simple cycle in the fully augmented graph contains at most one candidate, then R_A is generated by old R and the individual single-edge short cycles. Eliminate the independent new-edge coordinates: (R_A intersect Z)/R=sum D_e. Gain is the rank of a sum of fixed subspaces, monotone submodular by dimension formula. Standard cardinality-budget greedy gives >=1-1/exp(1) of optimum (at each step some remaining optimal item has marginal at least remaining gap divided by budget; iterate). No unconditional greedy approximation asserted.

## 7. Restricted NP-completeness
Set Cover universe U nonempty, m>=1 sets S_j and budget t. Two hubs a,b; independent element vertices x_i plus anchor x0; independent set vertices s_j. Both hubs adjacent to all x; a adjacent to all s; s_j adjacent to anchor and elements in S_j. Only additions b-s_j allowed. Clique complex is union of two cones at a,b: every simplex belongs to at least one cone (no a-b edge). Intersection consists of elements, anchor and selected sets with their incidence edges. Each covered element connects to anchor, uncovered elements isolated. Reduced Mayer-Vietoris identifies H1 with reduced H0 of intersection, of dimension number uncovered. Thus complete recovery at radius one under budget equivalent to Set Cover. NP membership for fixed finite field by incidence and triangle ranks.

## 8. Unrestricted NP-completeness
Instance U nonempty, m>=1, 1<=t<=m; remaining trivial instances can be decided directly and mapped to fixed yes/no graphs. Construct b, a clique of m set vertices, L=2t+1 layers each with hub a_l and independent element vertices x_(i,l) plus anchor x_(0,l). Edges: a_l to every set vertex and every element/anchor of its layer; b to every element/anchor in every layer; s_j to all anchors and to element i in each layer iff i in S_j. No other edges. All missing edges admissible; size polynomial since t<=m.

Forward: for a cover J insert b-s_j for j in J. In clique complex minus b each element/anchor vertex is dominated by its own layer hub (closed neighborhood contained in hub neighborhood); deleting it is a deformation retraction, since its link is a cone. After all deletions the remaining complex is a cone at any set vertex. Link of b is connected: selected sets form a nonempty clique, anchors adjacent to every selected set, each element adjacent to a selected set. Union of deletion and closed star has intersection link; Mayer-Vietoris gives H1=0.

Reverse: for arbitrary <=t added edges extract J from b-s_j additions. At most 2t layers contain endpoints of additions at their layer-specific vertices; choose an untouched layer. If i uncovered, x_(i,l) is isolated in link(b): no hub adjacency to b, no selected incident set, no new incident edge. Anchor in same layer is a different vertex of link so reduced H0(link) nonzero. Deletion of b remains connected (original deletion connected via common set vertices), closed star connected. Mayer-Vietoris gives surjection H1(augmented complex)->reduced H0(link), contradiction. Thus extraction gives a cover of size <=t. Radius-one equality identifies H1=0 with hidden entropy zero. Fixed finite field membership in NP: enumerate triangles and compute ranks in polynomial time. This is NP-completeness of the decision problem, not a separation of complexity classes. Independent review and prior-art search required.
